Section 1

Two ways to judge a biotech

Clinics and investors look at the same company with different goals. Runwise tries to respect both.

What research clinics look for

They ask if a study can be run safely and finished.

  • Safety proof from lab and animal work before humans are dosed
  • Clear, measurable endpoints like tumor size or a biomarker
  • Simple trial rules and patients who can actually be enrolled
  • A sponsor with enough cash to pay sites and survive delays
  • Regulatory basics in place, such as an IND and IRB approval

What investors look for

They ask if the company can earn a return.

  • The science and how the drug works at a molecular level
  • Data quality: sample size and statistical significance
  • Pipeline depth, so one failure does not sink the company
  • Market size and unmet medical need
  • Cash runway and burn rate before more dilution
  • Patent protection and exclusivity
  • Risk adjusted value (rNPV), which weighs future cash by odds of success

Where they overlap

Both sides care about cash runway and pipeline depth. A clinic does not want a sponsor that runs dry mid trial. An investor does not want to be diluted. That overlap is why those two ideas carry most of the weight in this model.

Section 2

Methodology

Each pillar is scored 0 to 100. The final score is a weighted average. Higher means lower risk.

Financial health

runway = cash / (quarterly burn / 3)
Score = runway divided by 36 months, capped at 100. Burn uses operating cash use where found. Beam uses net loss as a proxy.

Pipeline and product maturity

Stage 1 to 5 (50%), number of programs (20%), platform and team rating (30%).

Industry outlook

A 1 to 10 judgment of demand and funding trends for the company's market, times 10.

Section 5

The capex lens

Capex is money spent on equipment and buildings. For a biotech it shows how much cash goes into physical capacity instead of research. Heavy capex can shorten runway fast. Low capex can mean a lean lab or a company that outsources.

Section 6

Limits

  • Runway ignores new fundraising, partner payments, and dilution.
  • Stage, program count, platform rating, and industry outlook are judgment calls, not reported facts.
  • Two of the four built-in companies sell tools, not drugs, so stage means launch readiness for them.
  • Four companies are built in. Live lookup fills cash and burn from SEC filings, and the four judgment ratings are always entered by hand.
  • Built in figures reflect Q2 2026 filings. Live lookup uses each company's latest filing on record, and prices are delayed.
